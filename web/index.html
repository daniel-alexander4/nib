Set up Nib

Nib protects sensitive information — your signature images, signing identity, and saved files — in an encrypted vault on this computer.

An SSH key locks and unlocks that vault: a small key file on your machine, so there is no password to remember.

Your SSH key becomes the only way in — if you lose it, the vault (including your signing identity) cannot be recovered.

Reduce file size

Optimize is lossless — it removes redundant data and keeps selectable text, but mostly helps bloated files (little effect on scans). Compress re-renders every page as a JPEG image: big savings on scans, but it flattens the document — selectable text and search are lost.

Timestamp with OpenTimestamps

Creates a proof (a small .ots file) that this exact document existed, unaltered, at this moment — by anchoring it to the Bitcoin blockchain. Only a SHA-256 hash of the document is sent to public OpenTimestamps calendar servers over the internet; the document itself never leaves your machine. Keep the .ots file alongside this exact PDF.

The proof becomes fully verifiable a few hours after the next Bitcoin block confirms it. Anyone can then verify it with any OpenTimestamps tool — no account, no certificate, and no trust in Nib required. It proves when the document existed, not who wrote or signed it.

Verify a timestamp

Check an OpenTimestamps proof (.ots) against the open document: confirm the document is the one it was made for, and read back the Bitcoin block time it was anchored at. Only a public block height is looked up over the internet — never the document or its hash. The block is confirmed against several public block explorers, which must agree. If a still-pending proof has since been confirmed, you can save the now-complete, self-contained proof.

Hidden content

Scanning is best-effort — it can't detect deliberately concealed or steganographic data, so a clean result is not a guarantee. Strip neutralises auto-run code, scripts and risky links while keeping the page; Remove files & media only deletes attachments and media, leaving all else intact; Flatten turns pages into images — the one certain way to remove anything hidden, at the cost of selectable text. Password-protected PDF

This PDF needs a password to open. Enter it to unlock the document — the working copy becomes unprotected so you can view and edit it (saving keeps it unprotected).

Nib verifies integrity — that a document is unchanged since it was signed. Identities are self-generated, not vouched for by a certificate authority, so a signature proves a key-holder signed it, not who they legally are.           About Nib

          Version — · project home

          Nib fills, signs, and redacts PDFs entirely on this computer. Your documents are never uploaded.

          What a Nib signature proves

          When you Finalize & sign, Nib certifies the document so any later change is detectable. A valid signature shows three things:

          • Tamper-evidence — the document hasn't changed since it was signed.
          • Key-holder — it was signed by whoever holds your signing key.
          • Time — when it was signed (independently verifiable if you add a timestamp authority).

          What it doesn't prove

          Your signing identity is self-generated on this machine — it is not vouched for by a certificate authority or any third party. So a Nib signature does not establish your legal identity to someone who doesn't already trust your key, and it is not a qualified electronic signature (QES). It proves the document is intact and was signed by your key — not who you are to a stranger.

          Working with several documents

          Opening a document adds it — the one you had stays open. A strip of tabs appears above the page once there are two; click one to switch. Each document keeps its own page, zoom, scroll position and typed values while you are on another.

          Close view puts down the document you are looking at and moves to the next tab; Close all puts down every one. With a single document open there is just Close. Reloading this window brings every open document back — they live in the Nib process on your computer, not in the page. Nib holds eight documents, or 512 MB of them, whichever comes first.
