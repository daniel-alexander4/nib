    What a Nib signature proves

    When you Finalize & sign, Nib certifies the document so any later change is detectable. A valid signature shows three things:

    • Tamper-evidence — the document hasn't changed since it was signed.
    • Key-holder — it was signed by whoever holds your signing key.
    • Time — when it was signed (independently verifiable if you add a timestamp authority).

    What it doesn't prove

    Your signing identity is self-generated on this machine — it is not vouched for by a certificate authority or any third party. So a Nib signature does not establish your legal identity to someone who doesn't already trust your key, and it is not a qualified electronic signature (QES). It proves the document is intact and was signed by your key — not who you are to a stranger.

    Co-signing with other people

    A document can be signed by two or more people. Each signature names the one party that signer accepted, in a visible block on the page. Where two parties sign together, each names the other; where more than two do, the parties are the ones named by whoever convened the proceeding — so signers who never connected to each other have not checked each other's identity, and no signature claims they did.

    The same trust limits apply to every signature on a co-signed document: each proves its own signer held their key and that the document has not changed since, and none of them establishes anyone's legal identity to a stranger.

    Working with several documents

    Opening a document adds it — the one you had stays open. A strip of tabs sits above the page, one for each open document; click one to switch. Each document keeps its own page, zoom, scroll position and typed values while you are on another.

    The × on a tab closes that document and moves to the next one. With two or more open, Close all at the end of the tabs closes every one. Reloading this window brings every open document back — they live in the Nib process on your computer, not in the page. Nib holds eight documents, or 512 MB of them, whichever comes first.

    Signing
    
          
    Start or join a ceremony
    
          
    A ceremony is one signing that several people take part in, each on their own
            computer: one list of people, one document, passed from person to person in order until
            everyone has signed. It is meant for three or more; two people can simply co-sign.
    
          
    
            
    Signing ceremonies are switched off on this computer, so none can be started or joined
              from here.
    
            
          
    
          
    
            
    Start one
    
            
            
    You are the convener. You choose the document, write a short statement of what is being
              agreed, tick the people out of those you have pinned, and set a deadline. Nib gives you one
              invitation for each person — each one different — and you send them however you like.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    Join one
    
            
            
    Paste the invitation the convener sent you; the box opens in the menu, under
              Signing Ceremonies. Accepting pins the convener’s identity and nothing else. An invitation
              lets Nib watch for your turn. It cannot be used to sign as you: signing needs your key.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    While it runs
    
            
    The Signing Ceremonies panel in the menu lists every ceremony on this computer:
              who is in it, your place, and what happens next. The document goes to one person at a time,
              and each signature is checked against the ceremony’s own record before the next person
              gets it. When your turn comes you review the document and sign, or decline.
    
            
    While a ceremony is live its document is frozen. Editing it, redacting it or saving over
              it is refused, because everyone else was invited to sign those exact bytes.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    What it will not do
    
            
      
              
    • The deadline cannot be extended and a person cannot be replaced: both are part of what
                everyone signs. A ceremony that runs out of time is convened again.
      • 
              
    • One document goes round once, in order. Nib does not sign in counterparts, where
                everyone signs an identical copy at the same time.
      • 
              
    • A ceremony that has ended cannot be reopened, and signatures already given on it cannot
                be carried into a new one.
      • 
            
    
          
    
          
    
            
    What the finished document shows
    
            
    That its signatures are intact, that they all belong to the same ceremony, and which of
              the people who had to sign have signed. It does not record how the ceremony ended —
              completed, declined, out of time or abandoned. That is kept on each person’s own computer,
              and a signature already given still stands if someone later declines.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    What leaves your computer
    
            
    The document, to the people in the ceremony, over encrypted connections to the keys the
              invitations named. To find each other when you are not on the same network, Nib can use the
              public BitTorrent DHT: that reveals this computer’s public internet address to strangers
              there, and may publish one small encrypted record of where you can be reached — never the
              document, and never who you are. Reach peers over the internet, under Toggle Features in Settings,
              switches that off.

    Timestamp with OpenTimestamps
    
      
    Creates a proof (a small .ots file) that this exact
        document existed, unaltered, at this moment — by anchoring it to the Bitcoin
        blockchain. Only a SHA-256 hash of the document is sent to public
        OpenTimestamps calendar servers over the internet; the document itself never leaves
        your machine. Keep the .ots file alongside this exact PDF.
    
      
    The proof becomes fully verifiable a few hours after the next Bitcoin
        block confirms it. Anyone can then verify it with any OpenTimestamps tool — no account,
        no certificate, and no trust in Nib required. It proves when the document
        existed, not who wrote or signed it.
    
      
    
        
        
        
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    Verify a timestamp
    
      
    Check an OpenTimestamps proof (.ots) against the open document:
        confirm the document is the one it was made for, and read back the Bitcoin block time it was
        anchored at. Only a public block height is looked up over the internet — never the document or
        its hash. The block is confirmed against several public block explorers, which must agree. If a
        still-pending proof has since been confirmed, you can save the now-complete, self-contained
        proof.

    Hidden content
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
        
        
      
    
      
    Scanning is best-effort — it can't detect deliberately concealed or
        steganographic data, so a clean result is not a guarantee. Strip neutralises
        auto-run code, scripts and risky links while keeping the page; Remove files &
        media only deletes attachments and media, leaving all else intact;
        Flatten turns pages into images — the one certain way to remove anything
        hidden, at the cost of selectable text. Any removal replaces the open document with a new,
        unsigned copy — save it to keep the result.
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    Accessibility (PDF/UA)
    
      
    
      
    
      
    Nib checks the PDF/UA-1 rules it can verify itself — part of the standard,
        not all of it — and says plainly which ones it could not check. A clause it could not check is
        not a pass, and a document that passes every clause Nib checks can still fail one it does not.
        For a conformance verdict use veraPDF, the reference validator, which Nib's answers are tested
        against on every build.

          Listen address (advanced)
          
                Leave this blank to let Nib pick a port and announce where you can be
            reached over your local network and the rendezvous directory. Fill in a specific
            host:port only when the peer must reach you by port-forward or over a VPN you both use
            (e.g. Tailscale). Nib runs no relay of its own.
                
          
        
                
        
        
        
        
                With an invitation, Nib finds the other person over
          the public BitTorrent DHT while this session is armed. It tries your
          local network first and publishes nothing if that works. Otherwise strangers on that
          network learn this machine's public address, and the ones holding your record can tell
          that one other address came looking for it. Without an invitation, nothing here touches
          the internet.
