Welcome to Nib

Nib protects sensitive information — your signature images, signing identity, and saved files — in an encrypted vault on this computer.

An SSH key is required to lock and unlock that vault. It's a small key file on your machine, so there's no password to remember. You can use a key you already have, or let Nib create one for you.

That key is the only way in. Keep it safe and back it up — if you lose every authorized key, the vault (and your signatures) can't be recovered.

Timestamp with OpenTimestamps

Creates a proof (a small .ots file) that this exact document existed, unaltered, at this moment — by anchoring it to the Bitcoin blockchain. Only a SHA-256 hash of the document is sent to public OpenTimestamps calendar servers over the internet; the document itself never leaves your machine. Keep the .ots file alongside this exact PDF.

The proof becomes fully verifiable a few hours after the next Bitcoin block confirms it. Anyone can then verify it with any OpenTimestamps tool — no account, no certificate, and no trust in Nib required. It proves when the document existed, not who wrote or signed it.

Verify a timestamp

Check an OpenTimestamps proof (.ots) against the open document: confirm the document is the one it was made for, and read back the Bitcoin block time it was anchored at. Only a public block height is looked up over the internet — never the document or its hash. The block is confirmed against several public block explorers, which must agree. If a still-pending proof has since been confirmed, you can save the now-complete, self-contained proof.

Hidden content

Scanning is best-effort — it can't detect deliberately concealed or steganographic data, so a clean result is not a guarantee. Strip neutralises auto-run code, scripts and risky links while keeping the page; Remove files & media only deletes attachments and media, leaving all else intact; Flatten turns pages into images — the one certain way to remove anything hidden, at the cost of selectable text. Any removal replaces the open document with a new, unsigned copy — save it to keep the result.

Signature details

Nib verifies integrity — that a document is unchanged since it was signed. Identities are self-generated, not vouched for by a certificate authority, so a signature proves a key-holder signed it, not who they legally are. Signing times come from the signer's own device unless a timestamp authority is configured, which fixes the time independently.

          What a Nib signature proves

          When you Finalize & sign, Nib certifies the document so any later change is detectable. A valid signature shows three things:

          • Tamper-evidence — the document hasn't changed since it was signed.
          • Key-holder — it was signed by whoever holds your signing key.
          • Time — when it was signed (independently verifiable if you add a timestamp authority).

          What it doesn't prove

          Your signing identity is self-generated on this machine — it is not vouched for by a certificate authority or any third party. So a Nib signature does not establish your legal identity to someone who doesn't already trust your key, and it is not a qualified electronic signature (QES). It proves the document is intact and was signed by your key — not who you are to a stranger.
